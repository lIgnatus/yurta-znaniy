Talk to Elena

How do children discover their homeland? Through play

To a child, a game is an adventure. Playing develops memory, attention, reasoning, observation and imagination while introducing the language, history and traditions of their own people and their neighbours.

Words come from pictures, history from patterns, and knowledge from play.

Children learn to value other people’s traditions, make friends, treat each other as equals and help one another. The games encourage curiosity and respect for people from different backgrounds.

A teaching tool for the classroom

“Approve the Knowledge Yurt educational board game for use by educational institutions in teaching the state languages of the Republic of Bashkortostan.”

Decision of the teaching council at the republic’s education ministry, 29 November 2023. All 22 votes were in favour, with no votes against or abstentions.
Research
The games are developed and tested at the Department of Preschool Education and Psychology, an innovation site at the Institute of Education of M. Akmulla Bashkir State Pedagogical University.
Advisers
The National Museum of Bashkortostan, the Akhmet-Zaki Validi National Library, language teachers and artists.
Education standards
The games meet federal education standards and support teaching and learning in preschool, primary and middle school.

Elena Nagimova, project founder

For over 25 years I’ve supplied preschools, schools and other institutions across the republic. As I gathered their requests, I realised they had no board games or cards for children to explore the Bashkir language and local culture through play.

In 2022 we brought together advisers from the National Museum and National Library, language teachers and artists. That was the beginning of Knowledge Yurt.

  • Regional winner of the national Business Success Award, 2024
